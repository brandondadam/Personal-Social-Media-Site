I skated seriously from about age 12 to 22. Not competitively — just the kind of skating where you spend whole afternoons in parking lots, session the same three spots until you know every crack in the ground, and feel genuinely accomplished when you land something you've been working on for weeks.

Then I graduated, got a job, moved apartments a few times, and at some point the board stayed in the corner of the room until I eventually donated it.

That was eight years ago. A few weeks ago I bought a new setup and went to the park.

## What my body remembered

More than I expected. The basic stance, the weight distribution, the instinct to bail a certain way — most of that was still in there. Ollies came back in about twenty minutes, which surprised me.

What did not come back: any of the impact tolerance I had in my twenties. My ankles are registering complaints I did not receive at 17. My knees have opinions. There's a distinct sensation of my body asking "are we sure about this?" on every attempt at anything above beginner level.

## The kickflip problem

Kickflips were always my most consistent trick. I could land them pretty reliably, both stationary and rolling. Now I cannot land a kickflip. I can flip the board fine. I can jump fine. The part where I put my feet back on the board is not going well.

I think the issue is commitment. At 17 you commit fully to the trick because you haven't yet built up a mental catalogue of all the ways a trick can go wrong. Now I have that catalogue. My feet are hesitating at exactly the wrong moment.

The fix, I know from experience, is just repetition until the hesitation goes away. That's the frustrating thing about skateboarding — there's no shortcut. You just have to do the thing until you land it.
