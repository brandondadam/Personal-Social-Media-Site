I've had seven distinct desk setups over the past twelve years. Here they are, ranked from worst to best.

## 7. The Dorm Desk (2014)

A pressboard thing bolted to the wall that wobbled if you looked at it wrong. One monitor at the wrong height. A gaming chair that destroyed my back. I thought this was fine at the time. It was not fine.

## 6. The Kitchen Table Phase (2020)

COVID, small apartment, no dedicated workspace. I worked from a kitchen table for eight months. The chair was not an office chair. My back still has opinions about this. Never again.

## 5. The Standing Desk Phase (2021)

Bought a motorized standing desk because everyone said it would change my life. I stood for approximately three weeks total across six months of ownership. Then I sold it.

## 4. The First Real Setup (2019)

First apartment with a real room for a desk. Solid wood surface, two monitors, the first chair that was actually designed for sitting in for long periods. Not beautiful but functional. This is where I learned that the chair is the most important thing.

## 3. The Minimalist Phase (2022)

One monitor. One keyboard. Nothing else on the desk. Very calming. Productive, even. I lasted four months before I started putting things back. Turns out a little visual interest is good for me.

## 2. The Current Setup (2024–present)

A door turned into a desk, which sounds janky but is actually great — the depth is perfect, the surface is huge, it will never wobble. Monitor arm. Good lighting. Still working out the cable situation.

## 1. The Platonic Ideal Setup (theoretical)

Large, warm-lit, one very good monitor, a mechanical keyboard I don't actually own yet, a plant that I somehow haven't killed, and the cable situation fully resolved. I'll get there.
