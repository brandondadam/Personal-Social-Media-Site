I've started and stopped learning to draw about four times in the last decade. The pattern is always the same: I get interested, buy a sketchbook, work through some exercises, make something I'm proud of, lose momentum over a few busy weeks, and eventually stop. The sketchbook goes on the shelf. Six months later I look at it, feel vaguely guilty, and donate it.

This time I'm trying to understand the pattern before I repeat it.

## Why I think I keep stopping

Improvement in drawing is non-linear in a way that's hard to stay motivated through. You make rapid gains at first — basic shapes, simple perspective, line control — and it feels great. Then you hit a plateau where you can see clearly that your drawings are bad, but you don't yet have the skills to make them not bad. That gap between taste and ability is where I always quit.

The other issue is that I practice the wrong things. I do exercises when I should be drawing things I actually want to draw. Exercises build skills in isolation but they don't build the habit, because there's no reward in an exercise that isn't self-referential.

## What I'm trying differently

Drawing things I actually care about, badly, instead of exercises correctly. I want to draw motorcycles and architecture and people on the street. So I'm drawing those, even though they look rough. The plan is that the skills will follow the interest, not the other way around.

I'm also drawing every day instead of in long sessions. Ten minutes daily seems to be sticking better than an hour on weekends.

Sketchbook is not on the shelf yet. Ask me again in three months.
